# Row the Endings: notes

A chart game for Wheelock's Latin. The child rows a Roman bireme from harbor to harbor by filling a declension or conjugation chart one box at a time. The boxes go down each column, the way the chart is recited (porta, portae, portae, portam, portā, then the plural). The game uses the shared frame in `latin-common/`: the top bar, the player chip, the Inspector, the players store, sound and music. It plays from `file://` or from any static server, including a GitHub Pages sub-folder.

## Screens

- **Who's playing?** Since 2026-11-25 the players are picked on the site's front page (`../index.html`, "Who's playing?"), once for every game, as the parent asked. The game opens as the player chosen there; with nobody chosen it sends the child there and opens again after the pick. The chip's Switch player and Change chapter go there too and come back (see latin-common/README.md). The screens themselves are still `LatinPlayers.screens` (latin-common's `players-fallback.js`, or Case Detective 2.2's `players.js` later); they show in the game only when the browser cannot keep the choice (no localStorage).
- **The harbor (home).** A dusk harbor scene sits under the title "Portus", with a lighthouse, the moored ship and glints on the water. Below it are:
  - **Tonight's voyage card.** Before sailing it lists the legs with a minutes estimate and a "Set sail" button. Partway through it says "Row on". When the voyage is done it shows the scores and offers "One more voyage" (a new voyage, planned from the progress as it is now; it does not repeat tonight's legs). Its subline is "Defend your laurels" when no recorded race tonight was lost (a dead heat is shared laurels), "A rematch with the Corvus" when exactly one was lost, and otherwise "A new one, from where you are now".
  - **The quay.** It has one ship card for every chart in play, current chapter first. Each card carries three step pennants: done (red with a tick), next, open, or locked.
  - **The three steps.** On a laptop, when the quay has four charts or fewer (grade 6 today), a small card under it explains Calm water, Open sea and Storm and the pass rule, so the quay column is not an empty wood panel. It is hidden below 900 px and for longer quays.
  - **Footer.** It has the sources link for parents and Settings.
  - **Inspector lines.** The Inspector greets the player and points to the next thing to do. When the player's chapter is past the last chapter that has charts, he says so: "Chapter 5's charts are on their way; tonight we row chapters 1 to 3."
  - **Grade with no charts.** Grades 7 and 9 have no charts yet. For them a friendly card replaces the quay.
- **Step picker (sheet).** Tapping a ship card opens this sheet. It lists:
  - Calm water, Open sea and Storm, each with its best score. A locked step is disabled.
  - Called boxes: six single boxes from that chart.
- **The voyage screen** (one chart at one step):
  - **Where we are in tonight's voyage.** On laptops the leg pips sit in the middle of the top bar. Below 900 px the bar has no room for them (they used to draw over the title and the player chip), so a small "Leg 2 of 5" tag rides on the sea strip instead, at the top right. While the Corvus is under the tag (near the far harbor, or moored there) the tag fades out, so it never hides the rival; once our own ship has docked under it (the result card) it stays away, whatever is drawn again (a resize); it is back on the next leg, and a screen reader still finds it.
  - **Sea strip across the top.** The sky changes with the step: a calm evening, open-sea clouds, or a storm with rain and lightning. Two layers of waves move across it. The bireme rows from the left harbor to the right one, with two banks of oars, a red sail with a brass emblem, a painted eye and a drum on deck. The oars stroke on each beat. A right answer in time makes the ship surge and throw spray, and an "in stroke" streak badge rides on the ship's side, under the mast (on our own hull, below the far water, so it never covers the Corvus, the leg tag or the race chip). When the drum beats, the Corvus races in a lane behind ours, on a far band of waves (see "The race" below): a smaller bireme with a cream sail and a black raven, a dark hull, a teal-green wale with a brass edge, amphorae on deck for its cargo, and a brass pennant when dared. A chip at the top center of the strip shows "Race on!", "Final stretch!" and the result ribbon ("First in by 3!", "Prow to prow!", "The Corvus by 2"). A chip never lies over a sail: when the Corvus's sail (or ours) would be under it, it is put at the nearest clear place along the top of the strip, never over the leg tag or past the strip's ends (`chipPlace` in row.js; see "The chips" under "The race"). When a leg races, its raven sail is kept in sight beside or above our ship: where the page has room the strip is taller and the far water rises with the Corvus on it; from 600 px wide with no room, both ships are drawn a little smaller in the strip as high as it was and the Corvus's lane rises; on a phone with no room the Corvus is drawn wide of our sail as soon as the race is off level, a hundredth of a stroke either way (see "Where the Corvus is drawn", "The tall strip" and "The snug strip" under "The race").
  - **The chart on parchment, in Wheelock's layout.** Column heads give the dictionary entry and meaning, rows are labelled Nom. Gen. Dat. Acc. Abl. (Voc.), and blocks are labelled Singular / Plural. Verb charts use persons. The current box is highlighted. When a column is a gender or a tense rather than a word, its head says so above or beside the entry, as Wheelock prints it: "M. and F. quis", "N. quid", "M. quī", "F. quae", "N. quae"; "Perfect laudātus sum", "Pluperfect laudātus eram", "Future perfect laudātus erō".
  - **The Inspector's lines name the column the way the chart does.** "Tap its ending, down the porta column first"; "laudātus is in each box. Tap the word that goes with it, down the perfect column first"; for the pronoun "Tap each form from the tiles. Go down the masculine and feminine singular column first". When the boxes move on: "Now the magna column.", "Now the pluperfect: laudātus eram.", "Now the feminine plural: quae."
  - **The side panel.** It is on the right on laptops and docked at the bottom on phones and tablets. It shows:
    - the step and drum button, and the box count (n / N);
    - the question ("porta · dative singular"; for the pronoun the column's own word and its gender, "quae · genitive plural, F.");
    - the ending tiles. Each tile is a wooden block such as "-ae", "-ārum", or "—" for no ending. Pronoun charts use whole forms. At the Storm the player types instead, in a typing box with macron keys.
  - **Right answer.** The tile flies into the box with an oar stroke and a splash.
  - **Wrong answer.** The oars clash (a shake and a clash sound) and the right form drops into the box in dark red, with a callout naming it ("genitive plural").
    - On a wrong tile the Inspector says "Oars clashed! That box is portae.", and on laptops the note under the tiles adds the name. When that note would make the page scroll (laudō and moneō at Open sea: ten tiles, a two-line note and the chart's own three-line note are 51 px too many at 1366x768), the chart's own note under it steps aside until the next box (`msg` in row.js; it comes back by itself, nothing is animated).
    - On a typed form that belongs to another box, the Inspector names that box: "amīcus is the nominative singular. This box is amīcōrum."
    - The box counts as missed. On the first miss of a night leg the Inspector adds that the chart comes back once more at the end of tonight.
    - At the Storm, a slip in the macrons alone counts as right and shows "Mind the macrons", unless it makes another form (see "Typed forms" below).
    - A typed "laudātus, -a, -um sum" is right; the box then shows the chart's own form (laudātus sum), so it fits.
- **Result card.** It shows the score as the papers mark it ("22 of 24 boxes"), passed or not, and a pennant being raised. It lists the missed boxes with their names and the best run in stroke. After a race, one line with a small raven takes the place of the best run and carries it ("Race won by 3 strokes · best run 7"; after a line that ends in a full stop or an exclamation mark the run is a sentence of its own, "Level race won! You out-rowed the drum. Best run 13."; after a line about the Corvus coming first it is "your best run": "The Corvus was first in. Your best run 9."). The line is brass after a recorded win or dead heat and parchment otherwise. Below 900 px, where the oars panel is docked at the bottom of the screen, a raced card is never taller than the card was before the race and its race line is always one line (`raceLineFit` in row.js): the next-step line is hidden from sight (the Inspector says it; it stays in the card for a screen reader); a race line that would wrap leaves its second half to screen readers ("First in! Now pass the chart!" without " The rematch is tonight."; "Level race won!" without " You out-rowed the drum."; `.short`), and if it still would, the best run too (`.tight`); on a card with no line for the race line to take the place of (no next-step line and a best run under 3) the whole line is for screen readers (`.sr-line`: the ribbon on the strip and the Inspector give the result). The Inspector's result lines are each at most two lines of his bubble on a phone, one from 600 px ("First in! Pass the chart for the laurels.", "Prow to prow with the Corvus: shared laurels!", "The Corvus by two. Rematch next time!"). The card is fitted again when the window is resized (a phone turned). From 900 px, where the card shows its next-step line ("This chart comes back once more at the end of tonight."), the race line leaves " The rematch is tonight." out (`.rr-more.dup`). In tonight's voyage the button is "Row on", with the next leg named under it. In free play:
  - after a pass below the Storm, the buttons are the next step (for example "Open sea"), "Again" and "The quay";
  - otherwise they are "Row it again" (or "Rematch" after a lost race or a dead heat) and "The quay";
  - the first passed level race won at the steady drum adds "Dare them: they sail light". Pressed, it moves the focus to the card's first button before it is disabled, and the toast (also read to a screen reader) says "Dared! On charts where you out-row the drum, the Corvus sails light."
- **Called boxes.** These are single boxes out of order, like a quiz ("porta: dative plural?"). The screen has the same layout as a voyage: the chart of the called box on its parchment, blank, with the called box marked "?", and the question with four whole-form tiles (or typing at Storm charts) in the oars panel, with a row of bells that counts the boxes (brass for right, red for missed). Each answered box fills in on the chart, so the chart fills up as the quiz goes on; a missed one drops in dark red with its name. One stroke each. Missed boxes come first. A box whose prompt would give its answer away ("porta: nominative singular?", "quis, quid: ... quis") is only asked when a chart has too few other forms. Tonight's called boxes are 8 when the voyage has more than one chart, and 6 otherwise. In free play the result shows in the oars panel, as after a chart.
- **End of tonight's voyage.** The arrival harbor with the lighthouse and a laurel wreath, the score ("81 of 84"), each leg with its step and score (marked "review" or "once more" as on the home card), the called boxes, and "Back to the quay". Under the score, the regatta: "You won tonight's race!" (one race), "You won both races!" or "You won all 3 races!" (2 or more recorded races, all won), "Regatta: 2 of 3 races to you!". A dead heat is shared laurels and is named, never counted as a loss: "Tonight's race: prow to prow, shared laurels!", "Regatta: 1 won and 1 prow to prow!", "Regatta: 2 races prow to prow, shared laurels!", or with a loss too "Regatta: laurels in 2 of 3 races!". With recorded races all lost: "Tonight's race: the Corvus, by a prow. It will be back tomorrow." (one race) or "Tonight's races: the Corvus, the closest by 2 strokes. It will be back tomorrow.", worded from the result card's own margin (no chart title: most titles have a colon of their own); the Inspector says it on two pages, the second "The Corvus will be back tomorrow." Each raced leg gets a mark after its score: a laurel leaf (won), a raven (the Corvus first) or "=" (prow to prow); when any leg has a mark, the other rows keep an empty one, so the scores line up. On phones the mark sits under the score, not beside it, so a long chart title keeps its one line (beside it, four legs made the card 65 px taller than before the race at 390x844; now it is 26 px taller, the regatta line itself). Only races on passed charts count; with none there is no line.
- **Settings (sheet).** It has five groups:
  - the drum: off, slow or steady;
  - the Corvus: Race, Dare (only once unlocked) or Sail alone;
  - the vocative row: as the book does it, always, or never;
  - in the Storm: type the forms, or tap endings;
  - macrons when typing: light or strict.

Keyboard: the keys 1 to 9 and 0 pick tiles 1 to 10 (a bank never has more than 10 tiles). Enter presses the button marked for Enter when there is one ("Set sail", "Row on", "Open sea", "Next box"), so one press goes on; otherwise it shows the Inspector's next line. The typing box takes Enter: with a form in it, it rows; in an empty box it shows the Inspector's next line. Clicking the Inspector's arrow during typing leaves the focus in the typing box (and a phone keeps its keyboard up). When the tile bank changes at a new column, a keyboard player's focus moves to the first new tile. If the page is hidden in the middle of a leg the drum pauses. With reduced motion set, the animations stop (common.css) and the game still plays.

Screen readers: a polite live region in the oars panel says each new box ("Right. Box 4 of 24: porta, accusative singular."); the Inspector's lines go through the shell's live region. The page has a visually hidden h1.

## How the game decides

- **Earlier chapters.** A class can be behind, so a player may be on any chapter from 1 to the grade's last (the parent's ask of 2026-11-26; the rule is latin-common's `inGrade`). The charts follow the course of the player's chapter (`courseGrade`): an 8th grader on chapter 2 rows Latin IA's charts up to chapter 2, exactly as a 6th grader on chapter 2 does; an 8th grader on chapter 5 rows up to chapter 3 and is told chapter 5's charts are on their way; on chapters 9 to 17 (Latin IB, no charts yet) the home card says "The charts for chapter 12 are on their way." with Change chapter. The chapter step's "Ready" marks (`filesIn` = `chartsUpTo`) cover every chapter from 1 to the grade's last.
- **Charts in play.** These are the `LATIN_CHARTS.charts` in the course of the player's chapter (`courseGrade`, normally the player's grade) with `ch <= min(player.chapter, last chapter with charts)`, current chapter first and then book order. At present that means grade 6 chapters 1 to 3 (5 charts) and grade 8 chapters 18 to 22 (10 charts: three in chapter 18, two in 19, one in 20, three in 21, one in 22).
- **Box order.** Boxes run down each column. When the blocks share their columns (singular then plural) a column runs through every block. Otherwise the game goes block by block (quis/quid). Vocative rows appear according to the setting ("as the book": the chart's `vocOn`).
- **Steps.**
  1. Calm water: each box shows `s`, or its whole form if `given`, and the child taps the ending.
  2. Open sea: one box in every run of three is prefilled. The prefilled boxes are spread over the chart and never include the first box. The child taps the endings of the rest.
  3. Storm: a blank chart where the child types each whole form. If the typing setting is off, the child taps endings instead.

  A step passes with at most one missed box, and a pass unlocks the next step.
- **Tile bank.** The distinct endings of the current column, shuffled once per chart. From step 2 there are 2 extra endings: from the chart's other columns first, then from the same kind of chart among the charts in play, then from the rest of the player's grade, and only if those have too few, from the other grade (so a 6th grader at chapter 2 gets 2nd-declension endings such as -ī or -us as wrong tiles, never -ēbus or -uum). An extra ending is never one of the column's own endings, never differs only by macrons, and never makes a right answer when put on the base of any box of the column. A bank has at most 10 tiles, one per number key, so a column of 9 endings (laudō and moneō in chapter 1) gets 1 extra ending instead of 2.
- **Typed forms, macrons "light".** A missing or extra macron is forgiven (shown as a slip) unless the macron is what tells this form from another form. The other forms are the chart's own boxes and every box of the charts that share a word with it (`RowLogic.kin`: the three agō, audiō, capiō charts; the three laudō, moneō charts of chapter 18 and chapter 1; magnus with porta, amīcus and nauta). So *porta* for *portā* is wrong, *fructus* for *frūctūs* is wrong, and *ageris* (the present, another chart) for the future *agēris* is wrong, and the other way round: the Inspector says "Only the macrons! ageris is the second person singular, present passive. This box is agēris." A form that is another box's form, even typed without macrons (*amicus* for *amīcōrum*), is named as that form. One chart's note is about the length of a vowel itself, with no other form at stake: c22 ("the e ... is long after a vowel (diēī) and short after a consonant (reī)"). There a slip on that e (*rēī*, *dieī*) is wrong under "light"; a slip on the final ī (*rei*, *diēi*) is still forgiven (`MACRON_POINT` in logic.js). "Strict" counts every macron.
- **Tonight's voyage.** It is made once a day, and again if the grade or chapter changes. Until its first leg is rowed it is made afresh each time the home is shown, so a chart rowed from the quay before sailing is planned at its next step. It holds:
  - the current chapter's first chart that is not fully passed, at its next step. If every chart is fully passed, the one played longest ago comes back at the Storm.
  - one review chart from an earlier chapter of the grade: the one played longest ago, at its highest passed step (or Calm water). If there is none, a second chart of the current chapter takes its place.
  - every chart with a miss tonight, once more, before the called boxes. These are queued once and never re-queued from an "again" leg.
  - the called boxes.

  That comes to about 5 to 10 minutes.
- **The drum.** Off has no drum, Slow beats every 1.8 s and Steady every 1.2 s. In the Storm it beats one notch slower, so the default Slow becomes Off there. An answer before the next beat (plus 250 ms grace) is in stroke and adds to the streak. A slow answer is never wrong: the oars wait. **Changed with the race (all legs, raced or not):** the drum used to play three hits per box (`G.waited <= 3`, counted from 1); it now rests on a timer, playing while the box is younger than 250 ms + (3 + free beats) × the beat (`RowLogic.restMs`), so it rests at the same moment the Corvus stops counting. A box now gets three hits, four when it starts within 250 ms before a beat, plus one per free beat. Tempo, the in-stroke rule, the streak and the music duck are unchanged. The music is ducked with `LatinShell.duck(true)` while the drum beats. The drum, splash and clash are synthesized with Web Audio and follow the sound setting. The drum button on the panel changes the beat for that leg. Below the Storm the change is saved as the setting. Tapped while a form is being typed, it leaves the focus in the typing box (a phone keeps its keyboard up).

- **The race.** The Corvus from Ostia (corvus, corvī, m., "raven"; its hortator is Barbātus) races every chart leg, in tonight's voyage and in free play, when the drum beats at that step (`RowLogic.raceOn`) and the Corvus setting is not "Sail alone". Called boxes never race. One leg is one race. The numbers are `RowLogic.RACE`: GRACE 250 ms, REST 3 beats, MISS 4 beats, BONUS 2, FIRST 2.5, FIRST_CHART 1.5, CLIP 0.2, MERCY 2, HEAT 0.25, PROW 1, RUSH 700 ms, KEEP 3.
  - **Start.** Both ships wait at the quay. The child's first answer, right or wrong, sounds a cornu, shows "Race on!" for 1.6 s and starts the race; box 1 never counts, so the opening lines cost nothing.
  - **The race clock** (`boxCost`). With B the beat: a right box costs its time less 250 ms and less the free beats, between 0 and 3 beats; a wrong box costs 4 beats however fast. Free beats: 1 when the box starts a new column, and 1 per line the Inspector said at the previous miss (the correction, "We'll row this chart once more at the end of tonight", the rushed-tile tip; `missNotes`, `freeBeats`). At the Storm a macron slip the chart judges right costs a miss in the race only, and the slip line says so. Tile flights, the lesson after a miss and a hidden page never count.
  - **The Corvus's stroke** is one beat when tapping and two when typing in the Storm (the Inspector and the Settings help say "every other beat" there). When tapping it pulls on every drum hit; when typing in the Storm its oars pull on every second hit that a box waits (the first, the third and so on), and on the hit between it holds them and glides, so one pull moves it one stroke (`rivalBeat` in row.js; the race clock in logic.js counts two beats to a stroke either way). After it has stood still (the drum's rest, the mercy rule) the count starts again, so it moves off with a pull. It holds its oars while the drum rests; they ease up into the held pose (`oars-up`, a transform).
  - **Cargo** (`usual`, `cargo`, `amphorae`). The Corvus rows n + H strokes, H = round(min(u, 3 beats) × (n − 1) / stroke − n + 2), at least 0 (with a dare at least −floor(n/2)). u is the player's usual cost per box on this chart at this key (calm, open, storm, type): the mean of the last three races. With no race on this chart at the key, 1.5 × the mean of the other charts' usuals; with none at the key, 2.5 strokes; both capped at 3 beats. A race's mean cost is stored after it (finished, passed or not; never a called-off one), clipped to ±20 % of u when u came from this chart. H shows only as amphorae on deck (none, 1, 2 or 3), never as a number.
  - **The level-race rule.** H is 0 once u is under (n − 1.5)/(n − 1) of a stroke (24 boxes: about 2.0 s a box at Slow, 1.4 s at Steady). A child who answers every box in stroke always wins by at least one stroke and is never passed (a check.js property over 2000 random rows).
  - **Mercy.** The Corvus's share of the course never exceeds (boxes done + 2)/n; while held it holds water. So a loss is by a prow, 1 or 2 strokes. The first time it holds, the Inspector says once, on two pages: "Barbātus calls the Corvus's stroke." / "He never lets them lead by more than two strokes."
  - **Finish** (`raceEnd`). Child first: the margin is n × (1 − the Corvus's share). Corvus first: the boxes the child still had. Under 0.25 is a dead heat ("Prow to prow!": shared laurels, never a loss, keeps a run of wins), under 1 is "by a prow", else "by N strokes". If the Corvus docks first it moors quietly and furls its sail; the child finishes at their own pace. A dead heat is drawn as one: both ships dock with their prows level under "Prow to prow!".
  - **Laurels only on a passed chart.** On a passed chart the result is recorded (won, heat or lost, the run of wins, the biggest win, the Dare unlock) and the star or the cornu sounds 600 ms after the chart's own sound. On a failed chart the strip and ribbon still show who came in first, the line is parchment ("First in! Now pass the chart!" with " The rematch is tonight." or, in free play, " Row it again!"; the Inspector says "First in! Pass the chart for the laurels."), and only the cost is kept.
  - **The drum button during a leg.** Before the first answer the race is set up again at the new beat (or the Corvus goes home if the drum is now off). After it there is no race this leg: a race on is called off (the Corvus fades out, nothing is recorded), and a drum turned on after the first answer starts none (a race is rowed from box 1; it used to start one mid-leg and record a win by most of the chart). The toast adds " The Corvus races you next leg." only when that is true (tonight's next leg is a chart leg that races at the drum as now set), and " No race this leg." otherwise. Leaving the leg or switching player drops the race.
  - **No race.** At the default Slow the Storm has no drum, so the Storm does not race; once a day, for a player who has raced before, the Inspector says so, and that tapping the drum before the first form makes it race (on two pages) (the panel drum in the Storm is not saved, so for that leg only). With the drum Off on a Calm or Open leg, once a day: "The drum rests, so the Corvus rests in port."
  - **Rushed tiles.** A wrong tile less than 700 ms after its box appeared. The first one of the day adds a tip ("Think, then tap: slow costs less than wrong.") and one more free beat on the next box. After a miss the Inspector's pages are the correction, the tip, then "We'll row this chart once more at the end of tonight." (the last, as before the race). Typed forms are never rushed.
  - **Streak and final stretch.** The five-in-a-row cheer adds " The Corvus falls behind!" when the child leads by a stroke or more. With 3 boxes or fewer left and the race within 2 strokes, "Final stretch!" shows for 2 s, once.
  - **Dare.** The first passed level race won at the steady drum unlocks Dare: that card gets "Dare them: they sail light" and the Inspector asks. With Dare set, the cargo may go below 0 (it needs fewer strokes); a race is a dare only when it does (H < 0): then the Corvus flies a brass pennant, the Inspector says "The Corvus sails light: you dared them!" and a win is "Dare won by 2!". When the usual is not yet known on the chart (a fallback) or is slower than the drum (typing in the Storm), H stays at 0 or above and the race is an ordinary one, with no pennant and no dare lines. After a passed level race won at Slow by 6 or more, once a day, the Inspector suggests the steady drum, on two pages: "At the slow drum the Corvus can't keep up." / "Tap the drum first to race at steady!". The Inspector's Dare question: "You out-rowed the drum! Dare the Corvus?"; the toast after the Dare button stays 5 s.
  - **The Inspector at leg start** explains the drum through the race. A race line is never longer than the lines a leg had before the race (about 48 letters, 50 on the last page, where the bubble has no arrow and is 28 px wider): two lines of the bubble on every phone down to 320 px and one from 600 px. So the race's lines never make a play screen taller than the step's own lines do: 375x667 scrolls 166 px on leg 1 box 1 as it did before the race (164, 166, 164 on the step's own three pages), 320x640 193 px, 360x740 93 px, 600x900 60 px (play.js pages through every line and fails if a race line scrolls more than the step's own). The first race ever gets four pages: "Race! That raven sail is the Corvus, from Ostia.", "It rows one stroke for every beat of the drum." (typing in the Storm: "In the Storm it rows a stroke every two beats."), "Your first answer starts the race." and "Need time? After three beats the Corvus waits."; for a player who has rowed before they come first, before the step's lines, so the Corvus is met before the first answer starts it (a new player hears the step's lines first). Races 2 and 3: "Think, don't guess: a wrong tile costs four beats." A player's first typed race in the Storm (often race 4 or later) opens with "In the Storm the Corvus rows every other beat." (`told.type`). Later races name the cargo, "The Corvus carries smelly garum fish sauce. Race!" (then sacks of figs, a marble Neptune, a stubborn goat, a senator's luggage, jars of olive oil), a level race ("The Corvus has no cargo: a level race!") or a dare; a level race and a dare say so in races 2 and 3 too, in place of the tip. Again-legs say "Once more: porta with magna." as before the race and then, on a page of its own, "A rematch with the Corvus!" (only when the chart's first rowing tonight was raced). With Sail alone and the drum on, the first time: "The drum sets the stroke: try for one box a beat." In the Storm with no drum: "No drum in this storm, so the Corvus waits in port." / "Tap the drum before your first form for a race." A macron slip in a race adds a second page, "In a race macrons count: that slip cost four beats."
  - **Where the Corvus is drawn** (`rivalX`, `leadPx`). Below 600 px it is drawn relative to our ship, at most 3 strokes either way. Level is exactly level (the prows side by side). Off level it is drawn wide of our sail at once: 38 px back within the first hundredth of a stroke behind (`LEAD_NEAR` over `NEAR`), then evenly to 48 px at one stroke and 24 px for each stroke after, so at most 96 px back; 10 px forward within the first hundredth of a stroke ahead (`LEAD_FORE`), then evenly to 24 px at one stroke and 24 px a stroke after. The mapping is continuous and never decreases, a one-stroke lead shows either way, beyond a stroke it is as it was, and it may fall back over the quay. From 600 px it sits at its true place. On every screen it waits at the quay behind us before the first answer (where it can be seen; it slides to the start line at "Race on!"), and when it docks first it moors in the far harbor ahead of our prow. Up the strip, its lane (`.rtrack`) is on the far band of waves: its waterline is our ship's width × 0.205 above the strip's bottom (just above our waves), plus the lift of the tall or snug strip.
    - **Why so steep at level.** On a phone the two sails are about 23 px (the Corvus's) and 33 px (ours) wide and overlap in height by three quarters, so a Corvus 5 to 30 px behind has most of its sail behind ours, and one up to 8 px ahead too. The first version drew the first stroke behind evenly (0.3 behind: 63 to 69 % of its sail covered), the second drew 36 px in the first quarter stroke, which still hid it from just behind level to a fifth of a stroke behind (60 to 97 % covered at 0.05 to 0.2 behind). That band is where a close race rests: the race moves in whole strokes (the child's box, the Corvus's beat), and a child who thinks for three beats has the Corvus hold water a few hundredths of a stroke ahead of level (with 24 boxes, about 0.014 of a stroke per box of cargo), so a slow, even race sat in the hidden band for most of a leg. Now the whole band from a hundredth of a stroke is drawn clear, both ways; what is left hidden is level itself, which a race only passes through.
    - **What it gives** (the normal 58 px strip; the most over the ships' bob). By a pixel count of the raven sail (scratch scripts of the review), 390x844, Calm water: 25 % covered at a hundredth of a stroke behind, 24 % at 0.05, 22 % at 0.1, 16 % at 0.3, 10 % at 0.5, 9 % at 1; 11 % at a hundredth ahead, 6 % at 0.1 ahead; level 54 % (47 % on average over the bob). Open sea: 27 % and 16 % at a hundredth behind and ahead, level 61 %. The Storm: 25 % and 22 %, level 67 % (45 % on average). 320x640 is the same within a point (our ship is 96 px on every phone). By play.js's box measure, Calm water: 8 % at 0.3 behind, 2 % at 0.5, 0 % at 1 and ahead, at most 17 % next to level (a hundredth behind), level 43 %; the Storm: 12, 8, 0 %, at most 23 % next to level, level 61 %. In a simulation of the race clock a child at 5.5 s a box at the steady drum had a stretch of over 3 s with the sail hidden in 14 % of races (87 % before); time spent hidden fell from as much as 20 % of a race to 1 % or less.
    - **Still behind our sail on a phone with no room:** a Corvus exactly level with us (the prows side by side: about half of its sail at Calm water, more as the ships pitch at Open sea and in the Storm) and one within a few thousandths of a stroke of level (from about 0.003 ahead to 0.009 behind). Level must be level, and the strip has no headroom to lift the lane, so this stays. Also there: the top 5 px of the Corvus's box is above the strip's inner top edge, so its mast tip and small flag (and a dare's brass pennant) are cut and its sail's top touches the edge. A lower lane or a smaller Corvus would put more of the raven sail behind ours, and a taller strip would add scroll on small phones, so it is left.
  - **The chips** (`chip`, `chipPlace` in row.js). The chip's place is the top center of the strip (on a narrow phone with the leg tag, 38 px left of it). When it is shown, on a resize and when the strip is fitted, `chipPlace` checks the place against where the ships are going (not where they are in mid glide): the Corvus's sail, our sail, and with less weight the whole Corvus. If any is under it, the chip takes the nearest place along the top of the strip with the least under it, between the strip's left end and the leg tag (or the right end). So the result ribbon no longer lies over a beaten Corvus's sail on a phone (it covered 24 % of it at 390 px, 83 % at 360, 80 to 87 % at 320; now 0 %), and "Final stretch!" keeps off a Corvus two or three strokes behind (0 % of either sail at 390 and 320 px at every lead from 3 behind to 2 ahead). At 320 px the strip is too narrow to clear the whole Corvus too: there the ribbon may lie over its stern, never its sail. Its `left` is set at once, never animated.
  - **The tall strip** (`fitSea` and `fitRoom` in row.js, `.sea.tall` in row.css). In a close race the Corvus rows just behind our ship, and with its lane only a little above ours, our sail hid most of its sail (up to about 75 % at a quarter to half a stroke behind). So when a leg races and the page has room, the strip grows by `--grow` and the far water, the sky's horizon and the Corvus's lane rise by `--lift`: it rows higher up the strip, farther out at sea, its raven sail above our ship. Only heights change; where it is drawn across the strip (`rivalX`) does not. `fitSea` chooses, for each raced leg: the largest tall step that fits; else, from 600 px wide, the snug strip (below); else, on a phone, the strip as it was (there the Corvus is drawn wide of our sail, above).
    - **Steps.** +40, 32, 24, 16 or 12 px from 900 px wide; +34, 26, 18 or 12 from 600 px; +28, 22, 16 or 12 on phones. The lift is round(min(sw × 0.2, grow + strip height − sw × 0.592 − 8)), with sw our ship's width (150, 136 or 96 px), so the Corvus's masthead stays 8 px under the strip's top edge; a step whose lift would be under sw × 0.07 (too little to clear our sail) is skipped. In the Storm, where the ships pitch, a step needs a lift of sw × 0.09, as the snug strip has: that skips only a laptop's +12 (lift 11 px of 150), which takes the snug strip there.
    - **Room.** The strip takes the largest step that keeps the page from scrolling, with 4 px to spare, in two states that must both fit. While rowing: the bubble with room for two lines where it is narrower than 50 em, and on a laptop the chart's longest miss note under the tiles. At the end: the result card, drawn unseen (aria-hidden, visibility hidden) in a copy of the oars panel (a chart not passed with two misses, "Prow to prow! Now pass the chart!" with " The rematch is tonight." (hidden from 900 px) or " Row it again!", fitted as a docked card is, its buttons; in free play below the Storm also the passed card with its three buttons; on a leg that may unlock Dare also the card with the Dare button; the taller counts), with the bubble at two lines where it is narrower than 25 em (phones: the Inspector's result lines take up to two there) and one line elsewhere. The page's scroll height is never below the window's height, so the steps are tried, largest first, rather than computed; the view's 12 px slide-in is held at its end while it measures.
    - **When.** Once the leg is drawn (a 0 ms timer, after the shell has fitted the Inspector), when the race is set up again before the first answer (the drum button), and 120 ms after a resize (the shell waits 80 ms). It then holds for the whole leg. With the result card in place a resize (a window's edge, a phone's address bar) measures the end state alone, as it was measured when the leg was drawn, and never gives a taller strip than the leg was last fitted with while it was rowed: so the strip stays as it is unless the page no longer fits, and a window made smaller and then as large again gets its strip back (before, any resize on the result card measured the real card with the Inspector's line of the moment, and a tall strip could drop to the strip as it was). It is never animated and nothing new moves, so reduced motion is unchanged.
    - **No race** (drum off, Sail alone, the Storm at Slow, called boxes): the strip as it was. A race called off mid-leg (the drum button after the first answer) leaves the strip as it is, tall or snug, until the next leg or a resize, so nothing jumps under the child's hand.

  - **The snug strip** (`fitSea` in row.js, `.sea.snug` in row.css).     - **When.** With the tall strip: once the leg is drawn, when the race is set up again before the first answer, 120 ms after a resize. Never animated. A leg with no race has the ships at their full size.

## Storage

- **Players and the shared settings.** These live in `latinGames.v1`, which belongs to LatinPlayers and LatinShell. This game only reads them. It also writes `sound` and `music` through the shell buttons.
- **This game's progress.** It lives in `rowTheEndings.v1`, through `LatinShell.store`, which also drops the ids of removed players:

```js
{ v: 1, progress: { "<player id>": {
  charts: { "<chart id>": {
    best:   [ {got, of} | null, x3 ],   // best score at Calm water, Open sea, Storm
    passed: [ bool, x3 ],               // a pass sticks
    day:    "YYYY-MM-DD"                // last played (review picks the oldest)
  } },
  set: { beat: "off"|"slow"|"steady", voc: "book"|"always"|"never", typing: bool, macrons: "light"|"strict",
         race: "race"|"dare"|"alone" },
  race: {                               // the Corvus (RowLogic.raceRec cleans it)
    usual: { "<chart id>": { calm: [ms], open: [ms], storm: [ms], type: [ms] } },  // last 3 mean costs per box
    races, won, heat, lost,             // races counts every finished race; won/heat/lost only passed charts
    best: { m, id, step } | null,       // the biggest win
    run, bestRun,                       // wins in a row (a heat keeps it, a loss ends it)
    dareOk: bool,
    told: { first, mercy, rush, port, rest, nudge, alone, type }   // lines said once (true) or once a day ("YYYY-MM-DD")
  },
  night: {                              // tonight's voyage, or null
    day, grade, chapter,                // made again when any of these changes
    legs: [ { id, step, why: "new"|"keep"|"review"|"again" } | { called: true, why: "called" } ],
    at: 0,                              // index of the leg being rowed
    played: [ { id, step, got, of, missed: ["b.r.c", ...], mode: "tap"|"type", voc: bool,
                race: { r: "won"|"heat"|"lost"|"off", m, H, B, dare, rec } } ],   // r "off": no race or called off
    called: { got, of } | null,
    done: bool
  }
} } }
```

A box key `"b.r.c"` is block.row.column in the chart. The defaults are `{ beat: "slow", voc: "book", typing: true, macrons: "light", race: "race" }`. `RowLogic.settings()` cleans any stored value back to these.

## How to test

```sh
cd row-the-endings
node tools/check.js                  # rules: "All 2328244 checks passed." (about 45 s)
node tools/play.js 390 844 6 2       # phone, grade 6 chapter 2: a whole voyage with misses
node tools/play.js 1366 768 8 21     # laptop, grade 8 chapter 21
ROW_BEAT=off node tools/play.js 390 844 6 2                      # no drum: no Corvus, no race line
ROW_BEAT=steady ROW_THINK=1000 node tools/play.js 1366 768 6 2   # steady drum, 1 s a box
node tools/play.js 768 1024 8 22     # tablet: the tall strip at +18 and +34
node tools/play.js 390 844 8 21      # phone with 7-row charts: the tall strip at +28
node tools/play.js 1366 768 6 3      # laptop, grade 6: the tall strip at +12 and the snug strip (laudō, moneō)
ROW_THINK=2600 node tools/play.js 390 844 6 2   # a slow child: close races (won by 3 to 11), the Corvus resting near level
ROW_BEAT=steady node tools/play.js 390 844 8 21   # phone at the steady drum: a level race unlocks Dare (the card with the Dare button)
node tools/play.js 375 667 6 2       # a small phone: the strip as it was; scrolls 166 px at leg 1 box 1, as before the race (164 and 166 px on the step's own pages)
```

`tools/check.js` asserts:
- **Data:** every chart of grades 6 and 8 has `s + e = f`, and the right row and person order.
- **Boxes:** box order (down each column; quis/quid block by block) and box names. Sample names: "2nd sg. future passive"; *quārum*; *laudātī erāmus*.
- **Open sea:** the prefill covers a third of the chart, is spread, and never takes the first box.
- **Tile banks:** each bank holds the right ending exactly once and at most 10 tiles. Every wrong tile is checked on its own terms: base + wrong ending is not a right answer of any box of the column (not through the code under test). No extra is in the column or a macron twin. The wrong tiles come from the player's grade whenever the grade has enough.
- **Typed forms, in each strictness, with and without macrons,** judged against the chart and its kin charts, as in the game:
  - every form of the chart and of its kin charts typed for a box is wrong and named, also typed without macrons;
  - every form that differs from the box's only by macrons, in this chart or a kin chart, is wrong in light and strict and named (*agēris* / *ageris* both ways, *porta* / *portā*);
  - every missing macron and every extra macron on one vowel: a slip in light unless it makes another form or is the vowel a note is about; wrong in strict; the right spelling given is the box's form or one of its alternatives;
  - known cases: *porta* for *portā*, *portis* for *portīs*, *fructus* for *frūctūs* and *frūctus*, *amicus* for *amīcōrum*, *ageris* for *agēris* and back, *rēī* / *rei* for *reī*, *dieī* / *diēi* for *diēī*, *laudāta sum* and *laudatus, -a, -um sum*, *ager* `given`.
  - the kin charts are exactly the charts that share a column entry.
- **Called boxes:** the right form appears once among the choices; there are no duplicates, no wrong choice that is also right, no box asked twice per chart, and no prompt that gives its answer away (when the chart has enough other forms).
- **Records:** passes and best scores.
- **Tonight's voyage:** scenarios for grade 6 (chapters 2, 3 and 5), grade 7, and grade 8 (chapters 18 and 21).
- **The rest:** the again-queue and the beat.
- **The race** (about 88 600 checks): the race clock (grace, the 3-beat cap, free beats, never below 0; a miss always costs more than any right box); the stroke doubles when typing; who races (not at Slow in the Storm, not with Sail alone; Steady in the Storm races at 1800 ms tapping and 3600 ms typing); the timed rest; free beats match the lines said, and reading them never loses the race; rushed tiles; cargo monotone in u, 0 below the level threshold, never below −floor(n/2) with a dare; amphorae; a property over 2000 random rows that every box in stroke wins by at least one stroke and is never passed; a property that no loss is by more than 2 strokes; dead heat / prow / strokes; the final stretch; where the Corvus is drawn (on a phone: level is level; 38 px back at a hundredth of a stroke behind and 10 px forward at a hundredth ahead, a straight line from there to 48 px back and 24 px forward at one stroke, then 24 px a stroke; so over 2000 random rows a stroke behind always clears our sail and a Corvus ahead is drawn ahead; a Corvus resting after three beats on a box, a little ahead of level, is drawn at least 10 px ahead; it waits at the quay before the start and moors in the far harbor); the phone mapping as a property over 6000 random leads, half of them pairs as close as 1e-9 and a third at its knees: 0 at level and only there, never decreasing and strictly rising within 3 strokes, continuous (two leads never drawn farther apart than the steepest slope allows), flat beyond 3 strokes either way, a hundredth of a stroke behind or more at least 38 px behind and a hundredth ahead or more at least 10 px ahead, and the same through `rivalX` (the farther the Corvus has rowed the farther along it is drawn, with no step, on its lane); a macron slip costs a miss in the race only (judging unchanged); the usual with its 1.5× fallback and ±20 % clip; records (a failed chart stores the cost only; a heat keeps the run; the Dare unlock needs a pass at Steady with no cargo); `record` and `voyage` keep `prog.race`; `raceRec` cleans junk. Fixtures (24 boxes, column turn at box 13, Slow): a typical 2.5 s night with misses on boxes 3 and 9 (practice, first in); a 4.5 s bad night against a settled 2.45 s usual loses by at most 2; rēs and diēs typed at 5 s at Steady wins by 1 to 2.5 and skipping macrons loses; a fast child at Slow races level and wins big; a dare at Steady is close.

Ten more are right next to level (0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 of a stroke either way), where a close race rests; level itself is printed. From 390 px wide at least 60 % of its sail must show at each of the five leads and at each of the ten next to level in every mode; the tall and snug strips must show it at the five in between as well; at 320 px (the last part of every run) the five are printed, not asserted.
