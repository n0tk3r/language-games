# The music

The game’s background music mixes 9 slow movements by Antonio Vivaldi (1678 to 1741) with 13 passages of gentle music linked to the Sherlock Holmes stories (by Felix Mendelssohn, Jacques Offenbach, Frédéric Chopin, Richard Wagner, Thomas Morley in Frederick Bridge’s setting, Pablo de Sarasate and Johann Sebastian Bach). A second list, the show list, holds 15 of Niccolò Paganini’s 24 Caprices, or a part of one. It plays only in a game that calls `Music.setMode("show")`: Case Detective from version 2.2 on, while the Inspector plays his violin. All the music is in the public domain.

`list.js` names what plays, each piece file (`vivaldi-NN.js`, `bach-14.js` and the other Holmes files, `paganini-1NN.js`) holds the notes of one piece, and `../js/music.js` plays them (Web Audio API) on a small string band: every part of a piece on its own instrument, the violin, the viola or the cello. `../js/sampler.js` plays the notes from recordings of real instruments (`violin/`, `viola/` and `cello/`, from TinySOL). A piece waits a moment for the recordings of its opening (see Loading). A note whose recording is still not in when it begins, and every plucked note, is played by a small synthesizer for its instrument in `../js/music.js`; the viola’s and the cello’s are darker and gentler than the violin’s (less top, less bow noise, a slower bow), and each is about as loud as its recordings (`VOICE` in `../js/music.js`, measured with `tools/calibrate-strings.js`).

The Vivaldi movements have all their parts. The Holmes passages play the tune alone, on the violin (the Inspector’s own instrument), as the research chose them (`music/holmes/final.md`); no accompaniment is added. The Paganini caprices are for violin alone: they are the show entries of the site’s list until 26 November (commit f096ac3), cut and played the same way, each file renumbered.

## Loading

On a page with the music switched off, `../js/music.js` fetches nothing. With the music on, only `list.js` (where the page has not loaded it) and the first piece (a piece is 2 KB to 12 KB) may be fetched ahead of the first tap, so that the music can begin at once; no sound is made and nothing of the recordings is asked for before that tap. At the tap, if the page did not load `../js/sampler.js` itself, `../js/music.js` fetches it from its own folder, so the other games of the site, which load only `../case-detective/js/music.js`, get the recordings too. Then come the recordings: the violin’s (`violin/`, 3 files, 1.5 MB), the cello’s (`cello/`, 3 files, 1.2 MB) and the viola’s (`viola/`, 2 files, 1.0 MB), one file at a time with script tags, in the order the piece in hand needs them: the file that holds the notes of its opening first, a file it never uses last. While the music is switched off no further file is asked for (the one on its way is finished, so a visit whose first tap switches the music off fetches one file of recordings at most); switched on again, the rest follow.

A piece that begins from its top holds its first note back until the recordings that its first 3 seconds need have come in (a few of the 8 files: one for the first piece of the list, one to three for the others), but never longer than 4 seconds (`HEAD` and `LIMIT` in `../js/music.js`). With the files at hand or on a fast connection the first note sounds as soon after the tap as it did before (about 0.6 seconds), and it is a recording. On a slow connection the piece begins when the 4 seconds have passed, with what is in: an instrument whose recordings have come plays from them, the others from their synthesizers until theirs arrive (a note that began on a synthesizer stays there to its end). After a wait that ran out, no piece waits again until a file of recordings has come in. The 4 seconds are time waited with the music on and the page shown: with the music switched off or the page hidden meanwhile the piece does not begin, and it waits out the rest once the music is back. The same goes for every piece begun later from its top (the next piece of the list, Next piece in the Notebook) while its recordings are still on their way. A passage that begins inside its piece file (an entry with `from`) waits in the same way for the recordings that its own first 3 seconds need. A switch between the two lists never waits, nor does music that is switched back on in the middle of a piece. A file that could not be fetched is not waited for, and is asked for again at a later tap (at most every 15 seconds).

## The piece files

Each piece file calls `Music.define(n, { title, marking, ppq, unit, tempo, vel, pizz, slur, notes })` (see the comments in `../js/music.js`), and a piece of more than one part has two more fields:

- `parts: [{ name, inst, role }, ...]`: the parts of the piece, `inst` one of `"violin"`, `"viola"` and `"cello"`, `role` one of `"tune"`, `"accompaniment"` and `"bass"`;
- `part: "..."`: one character for every note (in the order of `notes`), the note’s part counted from 0, written with the same 90 characters as `notes` (`#` is part 0, `$` part 1, and so on).

A piece without `parts` is one violin playing the tune: the caprices are such files. The Holmes files name their one part (`parts: [{ name: "violin", inst: "violin", role: "tune" }]`), which plays the same way. The player plays every part on its own instrument, rolls chords and reads slurs within a part only (never across parts), and plays each part at the gain of its role: in the calm list the tune at 1, the accompaniment at 0.6 and the bass at 0.75; in the show list 1, 0.75 and 0.85 (`ROLE` in `../js/music.js`).

Every file carries its own number, and no two files share one: the Vivaldi movements keep theirs (1 and 4 to 11), the Holmes pieces have 13 to 21 (one number a piece; a piece with two passages has two entries in the list, with `from` and `to` in seconds of the file at its written tempo), and the caprices 100 plus their own number (101 to 124). A file’s name ends with its number. A returning player’s save names the number of the calm piece heard last (the game keeps a number from 1 to 24 and never saves a show piece). The site’s earlier lists used these numbers: 1 to 3, 23 and 24 (the first caprices, commit d335e54, 25 November); 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13 to 15, 18, 20 and 22 (the caprice passages, commit 0fb7079, 26 November); 1 to 12 (the twelve Vivaldi movements, commit d820d8b, 26 November); 1 and 4 to 11 (the nine Vivaldi movements, commit 88fd9cc, 26 November). A saved number that is in the calm list plays that entry (13, 14, 15, 18 and 20 are Holmes pieces now); any other (2, 3, 12, 22, 23 and 24) plays the first entry, No. 1.

## What plays

The calm list is the background: 22 entries, one after another, about 42 minutes before the list begins again. No. 1 first (the slowest), then a Vivaldi movement and a Holmes passage in turn. Nine Vivaldi movements and thirteen Holmes passages cannot alternate all the way round, so four times two Holmes passages follow each other, always by two composers; two passages of one piece, and the two Songs Without Words of Op. 19, are never next to each other. Every entry passes the calm rule as it is played (its rate applied): at most 2.0 note starts a second over the passage, at most 3.0 in its busiest five seconds, no trill-like figure, no fast run (no fast scale, no more than three quick note starts in a row), no fast broken chord; a Holmes passage that fails at the research’s rate may be slowed, to 0.75 at the least (measured by `music/mix/tools/measure-mix.js` with the measures of `music/vivaldi/choice.md`). With all their parts the entries have 0.37 to 1.71 new notes or chords a second, and at most 3 in their busiest five seconds. They play softer than written, with a darker tone and a gentler bow.

## Levels

The Vivaldi levels are those of the Vivaldi build (`tools/levels-vivaldi.json`: each entry brought to the loudness the choice set, by the loudness proxy, with the gains of the roles; calm levels at most 0.8). A Holmes passage is one violin alone, and the proxy does not say how loud that sounds beside a string band, so its level was set on renders (`music/mix/tools/levels-mix.js`: the game’s own player and recordings, offline; BS.1770 loudness): the nine Vivaldi entries at their levels measure -32.85 to -31.3 LUFS, and each Holmes entry was brought to their median, -32.22 LUFS, never above level 1 (the player plays no entry louder). “Zigeunerweisen, the slow tune” (-33.19 LUFS at level 1), “Träume (Dreams), the second half” (-32.45 LUFS at level 1), “Nocturne, Op. 9 No. 2, the opening” (-32.31 LUFS at level 1), “Barcarolle from The Tales of Hoffmann” (-32.51 LUFS at level 1) and “Barcarolle from The Tales of Hoffmann, the return” (-32.78 LUFS at level 1) would need more than level 1; they play at 1, a little softer than the target. The tone was measured too (`music/mix/tools/spectrum-render.py`): a violin alone has almost nothing under 250 Hz (0.1% to 0.3% of its energy, against 5% to 46% in the Vivaldi movements), and at the same loudness a little more above 2 kHz (the energy above 2 kHz is at most 2 dB over that of the brightest Vivaldi movement, in five of the 13 entries, all high tunes). The levels and every measure: `music/mix/tables/levels-mix.json`.

What was changed: the MIDI file (Mutopia) or the MusicXML file (PDMX, turned into MIDI by `tools/mxl2mid.py`, after its SHA-256 was checked) of each piece was turned into a short note list. Each note list is shared on the same terms as the edition it comes from (under the same license, or in the public domain when the edition is), and says so in its first lines. Written trills, mordents and turns play as the plain main note, grace notes as short notes of their own. In the Vivaldi pieces, where two parts of the same instrument play the same note at the same moment, one note is kept (the longer and louder); a plucked note is never merged with a bowed one. Of a Holmes piece only the line the research names is kept, and its fixes are made first (`music/holmes/tools/final_edit.py`; the edited copy is checked byte for byte against the one the research checked).

The credit line of `list.js` (shown in the Notebook) names the typesetters of the Mutopia editions and counts the MuseScore ones; this file and `credits.html` name each of them.

## Where the strings come from

Violin, viola and cello recordings: TinySOL by Carmine-Emanuele Cella, Daniele Ghisi, Vincent Lostanlen, Fabien Lévy, Joshua Fineberg and Yan Maresz (Ircam Studio On Line), [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3685367](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3685367), licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Trimmed, looped, level-matched and re-encoded for this game.

32 violin notes (G3 to E7 every three semitones, pp and mf; `violin/`, 3 files), 28 cello notes (C2 to D#5 every three semitones, pp and mf; `cello/`, 3 files) and 22 viola notes (C3 to F#5 every three semitones, pp and mf; `viola/`, 2 files). The player moves each recording by up to a semitone to play the notes in between. The list of the recordings and what was done to them: `violin/CREDITS.md`, `viola/CREDITS.md` and `cello/CREDITS.md`.

## Making this folder again

The tools are not part of the site: they are in the music workspace (`music/tools/`, `music/mix/tools/`), beside the tables and the scores they read. From `music/mix/`:

```
python3 -I tools/make_sources_holmes.py
python3 -I tools/make_sources_vivaldi_mix.py
node tools/build-mix.js <this folder> --report logs/build-report.json
```

The first two lines write the tables of the Holmes passages (from `music/holmes/final.json`) and of the nine Vivaldi movements (from `music/tools/sources-vivaldi.json`); the third builds the pieces, `list.js`, this file and `credits.html`. The order, the numbers, the rates and the show list are in `tables/mix.json`; the levels of the Holmes passages in `tables/levels-mix.json`, measured again (`node tools/levels-mix.js`, then `python3 -I tools/make_levels_mix.py`) only when a piece, a rate or the player’s mix changes.

The build stops, leaving the folder as it was, if a Vivaldi file is not byte for byte the Vivaldi package’s, if a Holmes file is not the one whose renders set its level, if a calm entry fails the calm rule as it is played, if a caprice decodes or plays otherwise than on the site before, or if two files define the same number. It removes the piece files that the new list does not name (`caprice-NN.js`, the Vivaldi files that left) and leaves the recordings alone.
