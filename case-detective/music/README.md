# The music

The game’s background music is by Antonio Vivaldi (1678 to 1741): 12 slow movements from his concertos and sonatas, and, while the Inspector plays his violin, one of 6 lively allegros (5 of them cut where a phrase ends). The compositions are in the public domain.

`list.js` names what plays, each `vivaldi-NN.js` holds the notes of one piece, and `../js/music.js` plays them (Web Audio API) on a small string band: every part of a piece on its own instrument, the violin, the viola or the cello. `../js/sampler.js` plays the notes from recordings of real instruments (`violin/`, `viola/` and `cello/`, from TinySOL). Until those have come in, and for plucked notes, a small synthesizer for each instrument in `../js/music.js` plays; the viola’s and the cello’s are darker and gentler than the violin’s (less top, less bow noise, a slower bow), and each is about as loud as its recordings (`VOICE` in `../js/music.js`, measured with `tools/calibrate-strings.js`).

## Loading

Nothing of the music is fetched before the first tap with the music on, and nothing on a page with the music switched off. Then `../js/music.js` fetches `list.js` and the piece in hand (3 KB to 23 KB each), and the recordings, one file after the other with script tags, the low register of each instrument first: the violin’s (`violin/`, 3 files, 1.5 MB), the cello’s (`cello/`, 3 files, 1.2 MB) and the viola’s (`viola/`, 2 files, 1.0 MB). If the page did not load `../js/sampler.js` itself, `../js/music.js` first fetches it from its own folder, so the other games of the site, which load only `../case-detective/js/music.js`, get the recordings too. The first note does not wait for any of it. A file that could not be fetched is asked for again at a later tap (at most every 15 seconds).

## The piece files

Each piece file calls `Music.define(n, { title, marking, ppq, unit, tempo, vel, pizz, slur, notes })`, as the old ones did (see the comments in `../js/music.js`), with two more fields:

- `parts: [{ name, inst, role }, ...]`: the parts of the piece, `inst` one of `"violin"`, `"viola"` and `"cello"`, `role` one of `"tune"`, `"accompaniment"` and `"bass"`;
- `part: "..."`: one character for every note (in the order of `notes`), the note’s part counted from 0, written with the same 90 characters as `notes` (`#` is part 0, `$` part 1, and so on).

A piece without `parts` is one violin playing the tune, so the old piece files still play. An older `music.js` reads the new files too: it skips the two fields and plays every note on the violin. The new player plays every part on its own instrument, rolls chords and reads slurs within a part only (never across parts), and plays each part at the gain of its role: in the calm list the tune at 1, the accompaniment at 0.6 and the bass at 0.75; in the show list 1, 0.75 and 0.85 (`ROLE` in `../js/music.js`).

## What plays

The calm list is the background: 12 slow movements, each whole, one after another, the slowest and gentlest first, about 33 minutes before the list begins again. They were chosen for few notes, no trills (the written trills and mordents are played as the plain main note), no runs of fast notes and no rapid broken chords, and a soft, warm sound: with all their parts, at most 3.2 new notes or chords a second in their busiest five seconds (0.37 to 2.93 over the whole movement), and a tune of 0.32 to 1.65 notes a second (the measures of the choice, `music/vivaldi/choice.json`). They play softer than written, with a darker tone and a gentler bow.

Each note list is shared on the same terms as the edition it comes from (under the same license, or in the public domain when the edition is), and says so in its first lines. Written trills, mordents and turns play as the plain main note. Where two parts of the same instrument play the same note at the same moment, one note is kept (the longer and louder); a plucked note is never merged with a bowed one.

## Making this folder again

The tools are not part of the site: they are in the music workspace (`music/tools/` and `music/vivaldi/tools/`), beside the scores they read. From `music/`:

```
python3 -I vivaldi/tools/make_sources.py vivaldi/choice.json vivaldi/work/choice/config.json vivaldi/work/choice/plan.json tools/sources-vivaldi.json vivaldi/notes.json
node tools/build-vivaldi.js <this folder>
node tools/levels-vivaldi.js <this folder> --out tools/levels-vivaldi.json
```

The first line writes the table of sources from the choice and the Notebook notes; the second builds the pieces, `list.js`, this file and `credits.html`; the third measures the levels again, and is needed only when the pieces or the mix of the player change (then build again).

The build checks every part against what the choice counted (its notes and velocities) and stops, leaving the folder as it was, at the first difference. It removes the old piece files (`caprice-NN.js`) and leaves the recordings alone.
