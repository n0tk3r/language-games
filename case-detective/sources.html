Where the material comes from

A page for parents: the book and the school plan that Case Detective follows, and how the game uses them.

The book

The Latin in the game comes from Wheelock’s Latin, 7th edition, by Frederic M. Wheelock, revised by Richard A. LaFleur (HarperCollins; ISBN 978-0061997211). It is the textbook that Hillsdale College K-12 schools use from 6th grade on. The game goes through it chapter by chapter.

The order of the chapters

Hillsdale’s Latin courses split the book by grade:

  • Latin IA, 6th grade: chapters 1 to 8
  • Latin IB, 7th grade: chapters 9 to 17
  • Latin II, 8th grade: chapters 18 to 27
  • Latin III, 9th grade: chapters 28 to 40

Schools differ, so the chapter can always be changed: press “Change” on a player’s case files. A class that is still on an earlier chapter can pick it under “Earlier chapters”: an 8th grader may choose any chapter from 1 to 27. The case files then follow that chapter’s course, so an 8th grader on chapter 5 gets the files of Latin IA. Stars already won stay saved and show again when the class gets back to those chapters. A player’s grade is changed under “Manage players” on the first screen.

How the game uses them

  • Each chapter’s case files use only words from that chapter and the chapters before it.
  • The charts follow the book’s order of the cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, ablative, vocative) and its model nouns.
  • A use of a case, for example the ablative of means, is asked only from the chapter where the book teaches it.
  • The “Every noun” file of a chapter asks for the forms of the nouns in the book’s chapter vocabularies, from chapter 1 up to that chapter. The Notebook lists them under “All nouns”, each with its full table.
  • The names of the jobs in 6th grade follow a chapter 2 class test from a school that uses this curriculum.

Word lists

The word lists were checked against the University of Victoria’s Wheelock Latin Exercises (7th edition). Please check the printed book before a graded test.

Music

In the background you hear slow music by Antonio Vivaldi (1678 to 1741) and gentle pieces linked to the Sherlock Holmes stories. When the Inspector plays his violin, you hear Caprices by Niccolò Paganini (1782 to 1840). The game plays every note itself, with recordings of a real violin, viola and cello. The notes come from free editions of the scores (the Mutopia Project and MuseScore). The recordings come from TinySOL, a free set of single notes recorded at Ircam in Paris. Privacy
